### Notes

**SageOx** *(new)* — Seattle-based; building "agentic context infrastructure" for teams where humans and coding agents work side-by-side. The platform captures context from chat, conversations, and coding sessions and feeds it forward as a "hivemind" so each new agent invocation inherits prior decisions and intent. Founders are unusually senior for a seed: CEO Ajit Banerjee was an original AWS EC2/EBS engineer and founded XetHub (acquired by Hugging Face); CTO Ryan Snodgrass was one of Amazon's first engineers and led the monolith→microservices transition (35+ patents); CPO Milkana Brace founded Jargon (acquired by Remitly). Round led by Canaan Partners; valuation not disclosed. The thesis is adjacent to (not competitive with) the agent runtimes themselves — closer to the Mintlify "knowledge layer for agents" wager.

**Blitzy** *(carry-over correction — should have appeared in May 6 brief)* — $200M Series A at $1.4B post-money, led by Northzone. Boston-based; co-founders Brian Elliott (serial founder, ex-Army Ranger) and Sid Pardeshi (NVIDIA Master Inventor, 27+ neural-net patents). The platform targets enterprise codebases of 1M–100M+ LoC and runs "thousands of agents in parallel for days to weeks of uninterrupted inference." Cited a record 66.5% on SWE-Bench Pro at announcement. New investors include PSG, Battery, Jump, Morgan Creek Digital, Defiant; strategics from Liberty Mutual, Erie, and BAL signal heavy regulated-industry positioning (insurance, financial services, government). Headcount has doubled in six months. This puts Blitzy in the same "autonomous enterprise software" lane as Cognition/Devin and Factory — but pricing on legacy-codebase reverse engineering rather than greenfield agent flows.

**CopilotKit, Nova Intelligence, Sierra, Parallel Web Systems, Cursor, Cognition** — Status unchanged from May 6 brief; details preserved for context. The Sierra → SpaceX/Cursor → Cognition trio remains the dominant valuation backdrop ($15.8B / $50B+ / $25B targets respectively, all in the past 30 days).

**Cursor / SpaceX option (status drift)** — Today's notable update: per multiple outlets (Phemex, PANews, WEEX) Cursor has accepted SpaceX's $60B offer in principle but **declined to jointly develop coding models with xAI**, opting to keep optimizing its in-house Composer model (partly built on Kimi). The $10B "work together" alternative remains the floor; SpaceX owes a $10B breakup fee if it doesn't acquire by end of 2026. Net: the structural overhang on a clean independent Series E close persists, but Cursor is signalling product-roadmap independence even under acquisition optionality. (Treat the "accepted" framing cautiously — primary disclosure remains the Apr 21 SpaceX statement; the "no xAI collab" angle is sourced to insider quotes rather than confirmed company comment.)
