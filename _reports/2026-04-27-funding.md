## Acquisitions & Exits

### SpaceX option to acquire Cursor for $60B (Confirmed — Apr 23, 2026)

SpaceX struck a deal giving it the right to acquire Anysphere (Cursor) for **$60 billion** later in 2026, likely post-SpaceX IPO (targeting June 2026). If SpaceX elects not to exercise the option, it owes Anysphere a **$10B payment** for the partnership work being done together. Strategic rationale: Cursor cited being "bottlenecked by compute" — SpaceX/xAI supply the infrastructure. For SpaceX, the deal positions it as an AI platform company ahead of its IPO. xAI was merged into SpaceX in February 2026.

### Windsurf (Codeium) — Three-way split (2025)

The defining deal of the past year, spanning three parties:

1. **OpenAI** originally agreed to acquire Windsurf for ~$3B (Apr 2025). Deal collapsed when Microsoft blocked full IP transfer.
2. **Google** agreed to a $2.4B licensing + talent deal (Jul 2025): ~$1.2B to Windsurf investors, ~$1.2B in compensation for ~40 hires including co-founders Varun Mohan and Douglas Chen at Google DeepMind.
3. **Cognition** acquired the remaining Windsurf product, brand, IP, and team; waived vesting cliffs for all joining employees. Cognition's ARR more than doubled following the acquisition.

### Meta acqui-hires Dreamer AI (Mar 23, 2026)

Meta hired the entire team of **Dreamer**, an agentic AI startup building cross-device personal agent infrastructure. Team joined **Meta Superintelligence Labs**. Dreamer is licensing its technology to Meta (beyond a pure talent grab). Undisclosed price.

### OpenAI acqui-hires OpenClaw / Peter Steinberger (Feb 15, 2026)

Sam Altman recruited **Peter Steinberger**, creator of OpenClaw (an open-source AI personal agent), to lead "next-generation personal agents" at OpenAI. OpenClaw remains open-source under MIT licence with an OpenAI-sponsored foundation. Meta and Microsoft also competed for the hire; Steinberger chose OpenAI on the condition of maintaining open-source status.

## Regulatory Notes

- **FTC** (Chair Andrew Ferguson) opened an investigation in Jan 2026 into acqui-hires across the tech industry, characterizing them as "mergers in disguise" that evade antitrust review. The Microsoft–Inflection AI deal is under active investigation.
- The SpaceX–Cursor option structure (pay $10B or buy for $60B) may draw scrutiny as a novel form of contingent acquisition.
